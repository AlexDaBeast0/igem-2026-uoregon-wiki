# Parts
**BBa_262TROOB (TmCphA1 - cyanophycin synthetase)**

Recombinant expression of the TmCphA1 enzyme in E. coli is meant to produce cyanophycin, a nitrogen-storage polymer. TmCphA1 can provide future iGEM teams a way to store nitrogen content in the form of cyanophycin. The TmCphA CDS is added to the iGEM registry.

**BBa_26Z03YM6 (Ammonium biosensor - GlnR-A system with GFP output)**

This plasmid regulates the expression of GFP as a function of ammonium levels. The higher amount of ammonium there is in the cell, the lower GFP expression there will be. The system takes advantage of two enzymes involved in the negative regulation of glutamine synthesis, GlnR and GlnA. The GlnR-A complex is able to inhibit its own downstream genes via the glnR-sensitive promoter. This glnR-sensitive promoter was optimized and can be utilized to regulate any other gene, such as GFP in this case.

## Flocculation

**BBa_26PFYJS6 ( E. coli coagulation- outer membrane vesicles - BCSB gene)**

Overexpression of the BcsB gene in E. coli is associated with enhanced outer membrane vesicle production and the formation of proteinaceous bacterial flocculations. This method of inducible cellular aggregation may be of benefit for future iGEM teams. The bcsB gene is added to the iGEM database with iGEM RBS/terminator overhang compatibility for Assembly.

# Wiki search

Every iGEM team starts by asking whether anyone has tackled their problem before. Each year hundreds of teams document their methods, designs and results on their wikis, but each wiki is a separate website, general search engines cover them poorly, and the only reliable way to find a relevant project is to already know the team’s name and year.

iGEM Wiki Search collects the text of past team wikis and makes it searchable from a single box on our team wiki. Results are grouped by team, so a search returns a ranked list of projects, each with a short summary and the specific pages that matched.

Features:

- Full-text search across teams and years, that searches the content of wiki pages, not just titles or abstracts. The current index covers 1,354 teams and 20,761 pages from the 2022–2025 competitions.
- Results grouped by project. Teams are ranked by their three best-matching pages, so a project that discusses a topic throughout ranks above one that mentions it once.
- Year filters and exact phrases. Narrow results to specific years, or put a phrase in quotes for an exact match. Words are stemmed, so “biosensors” also matches “biosensor”.
- Links back to the source. Every result opens the original page on the team’s wiki.
- Fully static. iGEM team wikis are static sites hosted on igem.wiki, so the search cannot rely on a server. The index is built ahead of time with Pagefind and searched entirely in the visitor’s browser, which downloads only the small parts of the index a query needs. Nothing is loaded from outside igem.wiki.

# Education

Three original curricula were designed and delivered through workshops tailored to three distinct audiences, creating engaging learning experiences for students with varying levels of prior knowledge about synthetic biology.

Grounded in student-centered learning, the curricula integrated STEAM (Science, Technology, Engineering, Art, and Mathematics) through digitally hand-illustrated workbooks. Universal Design for Learning (UDL) informed intentional pedagogical choices that prioritized student voice and expanded the means through which students engaged with content, encountered representations of synthetic biology, and demonstrated their understanding. Inspired by the DBTL (Design, Build, Test, Learn) cycle, the curricula were iteratively refined using both direct and indirect student feedback. Each workshop followed a scaffolded structure of lecture, activity, and reflection, allowing feedback to inform how content was introduced and taught.

Together, this UDL and STEAM-centered pedagogical approach as well as the adaptation of the DBTL framework to curriculum development offer a replicable model for future iGEM teams seeking to design accessible, engaging, and responsive synthetic biology education initiatives.

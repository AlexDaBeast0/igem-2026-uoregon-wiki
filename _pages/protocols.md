# Protocol: Electrocompetent cells

- Inoculate 50 mL of L-Broth with 1/100 volume of fresh overnight E. Coli culture (500uL culture)
- Grow cells at 37C shaking at 300 rpm to an OD600 of ~0.5-0.7 (best to harvest at early-mid-log phase; the appropriate cell density therefore depends on the strain and growth conditions)
- Chill cells on ice for ~20 min. For all subsequent steps, keep cells as close to 0C as possible (in an ice/water bath) and chill all containers in ice before adding cells. To harvest transfer the cells to a cold centrifuge bottle and spin at 4000x g for 15 mins at 4C.
- Carefully pour off and discard the supernatant. It is better to sacrifice the yield by pouring off a few cells than leave any supernatant behind.
- Gently resuspend the pellet in 50ml of ice-cold 10% glycerol. Centrifuge at 4000x g for 15 mins at 4C; carefully pour off and discard supernatant.
- Resuspend the pellet in 25 ml of ice-cold 10% glycerol. Centrifuge at 4000x g for 15 mins at 4C; carefully pour off and discard supernatant
- Resuspend the pellet in 10 ml of ice-cold 10% glycerol. Centrifuge at 4000x g for 15 mins at 4C; carefully pour off and discard supernatant
- Resuspend the pellet in a final volume of 1-2 ml of ice cold 10% glycerol. The cell concentration should be about 1-3 x 1010 cells/ml.

**NOTE:** The protocol says “resuspend” the pellet. This can be very hard by just inverting the tube. I recommend using an electric pipette or a P1000 or P5000 and just blasting the pellet with resuspension liquid until it breaks apart. Do not worry about hurting the cells. They can handle it.

# Protocol: Agar plates

Materials needed:

- Clean 2L plastic beaker with handle OR clean 1L or 2L glass bottle
- BD Difco LB Broth, Miller (white plastic container with blue top)
- BD Bacto Agar
- Plastic petri dishes (100mm normal size, or 150mm large size, or 60mm mini size)
- Magnetic stir bar
- IR thermometer
- Antibiotic 1000X solution

Procedure: (https://legacy.bd.com/europe/regulatory/Assets/IFU/Difco_BBL/244620.pdf)

1. Put a large magnetic stir bar into a: clean 2L plastic beaker with handle OR a 1L or 2L glass bottle.
2. Measure out 25g of LB Broth per Liter (50g for 2L) in a plastic tray on the scale.
3. Tare the scale again and measure out 15g of Bacto Agar per Liter.
4. Put the powder into the beaker or bottle and fill it with DI water to 1L or 2L.
5. If using beaker, place on cold hotplate and using magnetic stirring to dissolve powder. If using bottles, shake very well or use magnetic stirrer.
6. If using beaker, place aluminum foil over the entire top. If using bottles, place aluminum foil around the top cap such that you can take the top cap off and put in on the bench and the bottom of the cap will not touch the table, only the aluminum foil will.
7. Put autoclave tape on the aluminum foil.
8. Label contents and date on autoclave tape.
9. When putting top on bottle it should be loose, otherwise bottle may explode in the autoclave.(put tape over the lid so it doesn't fall off
10. Place the beaker or bottle(s) into an autoclave tray.
11. Autoclave the bottle(s). The duration depends on volume. Typically 1L needs 30min at 121C (not including ramp time). Larger volume needs longer time. You need to account for the fact that large volumes need longer time for entire volume to reach temperature because of the high specific capacity of water. Note that certain autoclave settings may cause the contents to boil over.
12. If using bottles, while waiting for autoclave prepare a 60C water bath. No need if using beaker(not really necessary)
13. Take tray out of autoclave (HOT! use gloves)
14. Make sure autoclave tape has dark stripes on it. If not something went wrong.
15. (you don't need to use a water bath, can just leave it out on benchtop)If using bottle place it into hot water bath, submerging about 75%. Don't let dirty bath water get near mouth of the bottle. Don't place the bottle into the water bath immediately after taking it out of the autoclave since it will still be very hot and can actually warm up the water bath. Wait for it to cool a bit ~65C. If using beaker place on cold hotplate, very low stirring ~40rpm (make sure stirring is not making bubbles).
16. Use the IR thermometer to check the temperature of the bottle or beaker.
17. Wait for the media to cool down to at least 55C to 60C before adding antibiotic. Using 1000X antibiotic add 1mL of antibiotic per Liter of media(if ur making 2L of media, add 2mL). When adding antibiotic make sure you use aseptic technique. So either do this near a flame or in a BSC(the hood in bacterial room).
18. After adding antibiotic magnetic stir for 1 min (<80rpm so no bubbles) after to make sure antibiotic is mixed in well.
19. Get your plates and start pouring the media into them, opening one at a time. Pour as little as you can. I usually pour not enough to cover entire petri dish then gently tilt dish so that media covers everything. Avoid bubbles! For regular 100mm plates I usually pour in stacks of 5 plates. If you stack plates it prevents water condensation on the lids.
20. Wait until plates have solidified and turn them upside down.
21. Let the plates dry at least 2 hr before using. Fresh plates can be left at RT for a few days or placed into plastic bags (these come with petri dishes) and put into the cold room or fridge.

# Protocol: Twist DNA resuspension

1. Before opening, briefly spin down the tube or plate at 400 x g to collect material at the bottom.
2. Add resuspension buffer or nuclease-free water to each tube or well to achieve the desired final DNA
concentration (C1*V1 = C2*V2). Ensure that the buffer and final DNA concentration chosen are appropriate for your downstream application. (~75uL water for 10ng/uL)

NOTE: Optimal buffer and concentration depend on downstream requirements.

1. Gently pipette the buffer along the tube or well walls to dislodge and resuspend any DNA that may be adhered to the surface.
2. (Optional) If the DNA does not fully dissolve after pipetting, or if you see visible material or particles in the tube or well, incubate in water bath or on heat block at 50 °C for 20 minutes to promote complete reconstitution.
3. If DNA will be used repeatedly over long periods of time, prepare separate aliquots to reduce the number of freeze/thaw cycles.
    - NOTE: Use or store working aliquots as soon as possible after preparation, and minimize exposure to temperatures above room temperature.

# Protocol: Transformation

1. Fill bucket with ice
2. Gather cells from –80 freezer(one with green handle)
3. Gather DNA from distribution(see other protocol)(NOTE: DISTRIBUTION PLATES DO NOT NEED TO BE KEPT ONE ICE FOR THE COURSE OF THE PROCEDURE. DNA is pretty stable at room temp, but for long term storage it should go back in the -20
4. Combine 1 µL of your resuspended DNA with 25 µL of your cells and place on ice
5. LABEL TUBE
6. Place plates with correct antibiotic in incubator so it's warm when you use it later
7. Mix gently and transfer to electroporation cuvette (between metal plates), place in cuvette holder and hit “pulse”
8. Immediately add transformation(WARMED) media(975 uL) (SOB) to the cuvette and transfer recovered cells to a test tube
9. Leave to shake in the shaking incubator for 30-60 minutes
10. Remove test tubes from incubator and rest on a tube rack
11. Label plates to correspond to the tubes
12. Transfer 100 µL of your transformation to its respective plate(100x and 1000x dilutions are best, so mix 1uL of transformant with 99 uL of sob for 100x, 0.1 uL of transformant with 99.9 uL of SOB for 1000x dilution), pour 10–15 glass beads onto the plate, close the lid, and shake the plate to cover all the afar with the cells (not the lid, just side to side shaking)(MAKE SURE PLATE IS NOT UPSIDE DOWN, SMALLER LID SHOULD BE ON THE BOTTOM)
13. Pour beads into a used beads bin
14. Replace lid on plate and place in the incubator upside-down (agar-side up)
15. Leave plates in the incubator for 12–16 hours
16. Time is specific, take out and parafilm the plates so they are sealed and place in fridge with the clear doors
17. Bleach tubes after plating cells

# Protocol: Innoculation

2. Transfer 5 ml of media into a test tube
3. If media(LB) does not have an antibiotic, you must add 5 uL of your desired antibiotic(located in freezer in middle slider on the right)

5. Eject pipette tip into test tube with media and antibiotic
6. Re-parafilm plate and put back in fridge
7. Place tubes in 37°C shaking incubator for 16 hours, then take out and store in fridge

# Protocol: Mini-prep

1. Pellet 1–5 ml bacterial culture (not to exceed 15 OD units)(don't really worry about this lol, almost never an issue unless you have something crazy) by centrifugation(16000 rcf) for 30 seconds. Discard supernatant, or liquid, and keep the sediment.
2. Resuspend pellet in 200 μl Plasmid Resuspension Buffer (B1) (pink). Vortex or pipet to ensure cells are completely resuspended. There should be no visible clumps.
3. Lyse cells by adding 200 μl Plasmid Lysis Buffer (B2) (blue/green). Invert tube immediately and gently 5–6 times until color changes to dark pink and the solution is clear and viscous. Do not vortex! Incubate for one minute.
4. Neutralize the lysate by adding 400 μl of Plasmid Neutralization Buffer (B3) (yellow).(LOCATED IN FRIDGE, MAKE SURE TO KEEP REFRIGERATED) Gently invert tube until color is uniformly yellow and a precipitate forms. Do not vortex! Incubate for 2 minutes.
5. Clarify the lysate by spinning for 2–5 minutes at 16,000 RCF.
6. Carefully transfer supernatant to the spin column and centrifuge for 1 minute. Discard flow-through.
7. Re-insert column in the collection tube and add 200 μl of Plasmid Wash Solution. Plasmid Wash Solution removes RNA, protein and endotoxin. (Add a 5 minute incubation step before centrifugation if the DNA will be used in transfection.) Centrifuge for 1 minute. Discarding the flow-through is optional.
8. Add 400 μl of Plasmid Wash Buffer 2 and centrifuge for 1 minute.
9. Transfer column to a clean 1.5 ml microfuge tube. Use care to ensure that the tip of the column has not come into contact with the flow-through. If there is any doubt, re-spin the column for 1 minute before inserting it into the clean microfuge tube.
10. Add ≥ 30 μl DNA Elution Buffer to the center of the matrix. **Wait for 2 minutes (INCREASES YIELD)**, then spin for 1 minute to elute DNA.

# Protocol: Gel electrophoresis

*THIS IS FOR 1% GEL, YOU MAY NEED TO ADJUST DEPENDING ON SITUATION*

*Materials*

1. PCR samples
2. 240 mg agarose
3. 30 mL TAEr
4. 3 μL SYBR Safe
5. 5 μL dye for each ladder and sample

*Procedure*

1. Combine 300 mg agarose and 30 mL TAE
2. Microwave solution in 20 seconds increments until agarose is fully dissolved, making sure to avoid boiling (a lower intensity setting can help with this)
3. Add 3 μL SYBR Safe into solution
4. Insert comb into tray
5. Cast gel into tray, gel should be less than 1 cm thick
6. Cover gel with tinfoil, wait 10–15 minutes to harden
7. Add 5 μL of dye to each 5 μL DNA sample
8. Prep ladders (1 kb and 100 bp) (4 μL H20, 1 μL ladder, 5 μL dye)
9. Transfer the full 10 μL volume of each dyed dna sample into a lane (if possible, avoid using the edge lanes and adjacent lanes)
10. Run gels at 100V
11. Weigh eppendorf tube and write empty mass on tube
12. Use razor blade to precisely cut out the band of the sample fragment
13. Store in marked eppendorf tube, & weigh again. Record full mass on tube as well.
14. Store in -20℃ overnight

# Protocol: PCR/GG cleanup

1. Add a 1:1 volume of Binding Buffer to complete PCR reaction mixture. Mix thoroughly. Note: For example, for 50 μL of PCR reaction mixture, add 50 μL of Binding Buffer
    - Optional step:
        - If the color of the solution is orange or violet, add 10 μL of 3 M sodium acetate, pH 5.2 solution, then mix. Note: The color of the mix will become yellow. A yellow color indicates an optimal pH for DNA binding.
        - (Optional) If the DNA fragment is ≤500 bp, add a 1:2 volume of 100% isopropanol. Mix thoroughly. Note: For example, 100 μL of isopropanol should be added to the 100 μL PCR reaction mixture combined with 100 μL of Binding Buffer.
        - Note: If the PCR reaction mixture contains primer-dimers, purification without isopropanol is recommended. However, the yield of the target DNA fragment will be lower."
2. Transfer up to 800 μL of the solution from step 1 (or optional step 2) to the purification column. Centrifuge at >12,000 × g for 30–60 seconds. Discard the flow-through.
    - Optional step: If the total volume exceeds 800 μL, the solution can be added to the column in stages. After the addition of 800 μL of solution, centrifuge the column at >12,000 × g for 30–60 seconds, then discard flow-through. Repeat until the entire solution has been added to the column membrane.
3. Add 700 μL of Wash Buffer supplemented with ethanol (see “Before first use of the kit” on page 6) to the purification column.
4. Centrifuge at >12,000 × g for 30–60 seconds. Discard the flow-through, then place the purification column back into the collection tube.
5. Centrifuge the empty purification column at >12,000 × g for 1 minute to completely remove any residual wash buffer.
    
    IMPORTANT! This step is essential as the presence of residual ethanol in the DNA sample may inhibit subsequent reactions.
    
6. Transfer the purification column to a clean 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube.
7. Add 50 μL of Elution Buffer to the center of the purification column membrane. Centrifuge at > 12,000 × g for 1 minute. Discard the purification column. For Golden Gate purification, I recommend eluting with 20 uL.
    
    Note: · For low DNA amounts the elution volumes can be reduced to increase DNA concentration. An elution volume between 20–50 μL does not significantly reduce the DNA yield. However, elution volumes less than 10 μL are not recommended. · If DNA fragment is >10 kb, prewarm Elution Buffer to 65°C before applying to column. · If the elution volume is 10 μL and DNA amount is ≥5 μg, incubate column for 1 minute at room temperature before centrifugation.
    

# Protocol: Protein purification & SDS-PAGE

## Setup

1. Get out buffers needed:
    1. “IMAC wash #1 / 50mM tris-HCl (pH 8) / 0.5 M NaCl / 30mM imidazole
    2. “IMAC wash #2 / 50mM tris-HCl (pH 5) / 0.5 M NaCl / 60mM imidazole”
    3. “IMAC Elution buffer / 50mM Tris-HC (pH 8) / 10mM TCEP / 0.3 M NaCl / 5mM EDTA / 400 mM imidazole”
    4. IMAC Resuspension buffer 50 mM tris-HCl (pH 8) / 100mM NaCl
2. Make lysis buffer (for 5mL tubes):
    1. (1X / 10X) * (1000uL) = 100 uL 10X Bug Buster
    2. (1 mM / 250mM) * (1000uL) = 4uL 250mM PMSF
    3. (1X / 1000X) * (1000uL) = 1.0uL 1X DNAse
    4. (1X / 1000X) * (1000uL) = 1.0uL 1X lysozyme
    5. Add H2O until volume is 1mL (894uL)
3. Lysis buffer for one 50mL culture:
    1. (1X / 10X) * (2000uL) = 200 uL 10X Bug Buster
    2. (1 mM / 250mM) * (2000uL) = 8uL 250mM PMSF
    3. (1X / 1000X) * (2000uL) = 2.0uL 1X DNAse
    4. (1X / 1000X) * (2000uL) = 2.0uL 1X lysozyme
    5. Add H2O until volume is 2mL (1,788uL)

## Lyse cells

1. Add 1mL resuspension buffer (10mL for 50mL cultures) and 200uL lysis buffer (2mL for a 50mL culture) to each cell in their respective tube.
    1. Resuspend & vortex until all of the cell pellet is dissolved.
2. Place tube(s) in a shaker at 250rpm & 37C for 15 min
3. Grab cells from the shaker and centrifuge at 16,000 rcf for 10 min to clear the lysate. Don’t let sit between centrifuging and removing supernatant.
    1. 10 min at 4,500 rcf for 50mL cultures.

## Prepare Nickel-based IMAC columns

1. Gather translucent Biorad purification columns and holder.
2. Wash out columns with miliQ H2O (can speed up process by pushing down with pressurized air)
    1. Make sure to place some kind of tray beneath to catch the flow through water.
3. Vortex cleaned Nickel beads (from black 4C w/ blue label) and add ~2mL to each column. Let drip until fully packed.
4. Add 20mL of MiliQ H2O to each column and let it drip (this washes out the ethanol).
5. Add 20mL of your resuspension buffer to each column and let it drip (This equilibrates the column with your buffer).

**Bead Binding**

1. Pour supernatant from centrifuged tubes (”Lyse cells” step 3) into their respective columns. Ensure both caps are on.
    1. Save the remaining cell pellet here. This can be run on your gel to determine if your protein is insoluble.
    
    (1st sample timepoint for gel: remaining cell contents after supernatant extraction.)
    
2. Gently invert columns (which includes your cells, nickel beads, and resuspension buffer) and tape onto the shaker thingy in the walk-in 4C.
    1. Let rotate for ~45 min so that the protein 6xhis tag has time to bind to the nickel beads.

## Run samples through column

1. Place an empty falcon tube under each column. Label “Flow through”.
2. Remove the bottom cap and use gravity or air pressure to push supernatant until there is a small amount of liquid above the beads. Place small cap back on column.

(2nd sample timepoint: save flow through to run through spin column and then onto gel.)

1. Place another falcon tube under the column. Label “Wash 1”
2. Add 10mL of wash buffer #1 to each column, remove small cap, and let drip until just above beads. Place small cap back on column.

(3rd sample timepoint (optional: saved solution after wash 1 to run through spin column and then on gel.)

1. Place another falcon tube under the column. Label “Wash 2”
2. Add 10mL of Wash buffer #2 to each column, remove small cap, and let drip until just above beads. Place small cap back on column.
3. Place another falcon tube under the column. Label “Elution Buffer”
4. Add 15mL of Elution buffer to each column, remove small cap, and let drip until just above beads. Place small cap back on column.

(3th sample timepoint: run eluted/purified protein extraction through spin column and then put onto gel.)

## Concentrate samples

1. Acquire a 50mL pink spin column. Make sure the molecular weight cut-off is less than the size of your protein.
2. Add elution to the spin column and centrifuge for 10 min at 4,500 rpm.

If you want to compare wash steps during the gel, repeat this process using the Flow through, Wash 1, and/or Wash2.

## SDS-PAGE

1. Add 12uL from each spin column to a 200uL tube.
    1. If using cell pellet, resuspend pellet in ~500uL 1X PBS. If your 12uL sample from this isn’t taken up by the dye in the subsequent step, you may have to dilute sample accordingly.
2. Add & resuspend 4uL blue dye in each tube.
    
    Dye recipe:
    
    1. Laemmli = 0.9(((#samples)+2)*6)
    2. Beta = 0.1(((#samples)+2) *6)
3. Place tubes on heat pad for 10 min.
4. Add the remaining (~10uL) protein + dye to each well of the protein gel
    1. Make sure to label which well corresponds to which sample. Ladders in the front and back trays should be in different wells (this allows for easy sample identification).
5. Let run for 30 min - 2 hr at 70V-150V, depending on the expected size of your protein.

## After Gel

1. Pull the green sides of gel container open.
    1. Take out one tray at a time, being cautious to not drop the trays.
2. Pry open clear gel trays using green tool.
    1. There are arrows to indicate where you should pry.
    2. Do not touch the bottom of the tray. Only touch the sides.
3. Pour Coomassie blue dye (big bottle with foil) into Tupperware containers until it is high enough to cover the gel (1 inch).
4. Microwave Tupperware for 30 seconds.
5. Carefully slide the gel into the dye-filled Tupperware. Ensure it is flat and not folded.
6. Place Tupperware on flat shaker for 1 hr.
7. Once shaken, pour just the dye back into the bottle using a funnel.
    1. Wash off the funnel and wipe the inside of the Tupperware shortly after.
    2. Ensure the gel stays flat and inside the Tupperware. Use the lid as a stopper.
8. Bathe the gel in de-stain (10% ethanol / 7.5 % Acetic Acid).
9. Microwave for 30 seconds.
10. Place paper towels around the gel (this helps absorb any remaining dye).
11. Put Tupperware on flat shaker and let de-stain for ~ 1 hr.
    1. Check to see if bands are visible. If not, repeat 9 —>.
12. Remove paper towels and pour de-stain into any container, ensuring the gel remains flat and intact inside the Tupperware.
    1. Do not pour de-stain down the drain!
13. Pour this excess de-stain into a bottle under the sink in the front fume hood room labeled “[I don’t know yet]”
14. carefully slide the gel onto the yellow side of the imaging tray.
    1. Select: Protein gel with dye → Commassic blue orange tray

# Protocol: Cyanophycin purification

*Has not been run through in lab yet.*

1. Setup
    1. Make 1M HCl stock.
        1. Add 2mL of [borrowed] 5M HCl to 8mL of molecular grade H2O.
    2. Make 10M NaOH.
        1. Measure out 40g of NaOH pellets (used Hettiaratchi’s 500g stock).
        2. Fill up flask with 70mL deionized H2O and add a few NaOH pellets at a time.
            1. Keep flask in ice and stir while adding pellets. Could use magnetic stir bar, manual stir bar, or just spin the flask around with your hand.
        3. Once dissolved and cool enough, pour solution in a bottle and seal top. LABEL!
2. Resuspend cell pellet in 1 M HCl at a ratio of 10 mL of HCl per gram of wet cell weight (~**500uL** per tube for a 0.05 g cell pellet)
3. Vortex and incubate in shaker for 6 hr at room temperature
4. Centrifuge at 10,000 g for 15 min - remove cell debris & collect supernatant
5. Add Tris-HCl pH 8.0 to sample for a final concentration of 0.5 M.
6. Neutralize sample with 10 M NaOH
    1. Collect the precipitated insoluble cyanophycin by centrifugation at 10,000 g for 15 min
7. Add supernatant (soluble cyanophycin) to twice the volume of ethanol
    1. Collect the precipitated soluble cyanophycin by centrifuge at 10,000 g for 15 min
8. Lyophilize and weigh soluble and insoluble fractions.

# Protocol: Bradford assay

Based on: Russel McLoughlin’s Bradford Plate-Based Protein Concentration Protocol

The Bradford assay is a colorimetric method used to quantify protein concentration by comparing the color change of unknown samples to a standard curve generated from proteins of known concentration. It is less sensitive to imidazole than a NanoDrop measurement and generally provides greater accuracy. The assay requires approximately 10 µL of protein sample.

## Gather materials

| Name | Location | notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BSA standard Aliquot (Bradford assay ladder) | -20C Parisa Freezer. | • Green container |
| • Grab one 8-tube strip. |  |  |
| Bradford Protein Assay Reagent | 4C Left deli fridge. | Big brown bottle. |
| 96-well clear plate | Usually by Parisa sink | Pierce™ 96-Well Polystyrene Plates, Corner Notch |
| Own samples (either from pink spin column or in aliquots post-buffer exchange) | Could be anywhere lowkey. |  |

## Procedure

1. Pipette 10uL of each ladder (1-8) and your sample into the microplate wells according to the diagram below. Include 3 blank wells with 10uL PBS.
    1. Only one unique protein needs to be in one well.
2. Add 300uL of the Bradford Protein Assay Reagent to each well (ladders, blank, sample).
3. Place plate in [plate reader in tech room corner]
4. Go on the computer (near quibit) and click “New Read” at the top.
5. Shake the plate for 30 sec.
6. Let plate incubate at room temperature for 10 minutes.
7. Measure the absorbance at 595 nm with the plate reader.
8. Save the data as an excel file.

5. Perform a 1:50 dilution from starter culture to new culture. Add 1000uL of starter culture to the 50mL of new liquid culture.
6. Let cells grow until at an OD of 0.5-0.6.
7. Once liquid cultures are at the target OD, induce with 500uL of IPTG.
8. Let cells grow for 4 hr.
9. Harvest cell pellets via centrifugation for 10 min at 4,500 rcf in the big centrifuge in the tech room.

# Protocol: Directed Evolution

## **Materials needed**

- Plates/cultures from the previous day’s experiment
- LB
- 3 LB Plates (no antibiotic)

Note: For this entire procedure, try your best to use sterile technique. Spray every surface and gloves/lab coat sleeves with ethanol before beginning and if you touch anything not sterile. Work close to the bunsen burner flame when working on the bench, and if using the tissue hood, spray the surface with ethanol, and spray the materials you need before putting them inside.

- Gather the required materials and put them in the cell culture room
- For plates:
    1. You’ll be working on the bench, so spray it (and gloves) down with ethanol and light the bunsen burner.
    2. Grab an inoculation loop. If the package isn’t opened, they are sterile, if the package is open, use the bunsen burner flame to sterilize the inoculation loop, but be careful because they can melt!

    4. Put used inoculation loops into biohazard bin

# Protocol: Nitrite Quantitation by Griess Assay

*Adapted from the Invitrogen Griess Reagent Kit product information sheet (Cat. No. G7921, Pub. No. MAN0001938, Rev. B). For research use only.*

## 1. Materials

### Kit components (Cat. No. G7921)

| Component | Item | Amount | Concentration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | N-(1-naphthyl)ethylenediamine dihydrochloride | 25 mL | 0.1% (1 mg/mL), sealed under argon |
| B | Sulfanilic acid | 25 mL | 1% (10 mg/mL) in 5% phosphoric acid |
| C | Nitrite standard solution | 1.0 mL | 1.0 mM sodium nitrite in deionized water |

**Storage:** 2-8 C. Do not freeze. Protect from light and from air. Component A oxidizes in air, so reseal promptly after use. Large crystals may form in Component B and can take up to a week to dissolve at room temperature; complete dissolution is not required for use.

### Other materials

- Deionized water
- Clear, flat-bottom 96-well microplate (microplate assay) or 1 cm path length cuvettes (cuvette assay)
- Microplate reader or spectrophotometer capable of reading 548 nm (520-590 nm is acceptable if 548 nm is unavailable on a plate reader)
- Micropipettes and tips; microcentrifuge tubes for the dilution series
- Prepared samples (clarified supernatant, see Section 3)

## 2. Preparation of Griess Reagent

1. Bring Components A and B to room temperature.
2. Mix **equal volumes** of Component A and Component B. This mixture is the **Griess Reagent**.
3. Make only as much as you need for the experiment you are running right now:
    - 20 uL per microplate well
    - 100 uL per cuvette
4. **Do not store the mixed reagent for more than 8 hours.** Discard leftovers.

*Planning tip: for a full 96-well plate, mix about 2.4 mL of Griess Reagent (1.2 mL A + 1.2 mL B). That covers 96 wells plus reference wells and pipetting overage.*

## 3. Sample preparation

- Prepare a supernatant from centrifuged cell lysate, or collect culture supernatant or tissue perfusate. Consult the literature for a protocol specific to your sample type.
- Remove cells and debris by centrifugation before assaying; turbid samples will inflate absorbance.
- Run the assay on the same day if possible. Keep samples on ice until use.
- Include careful controls. Nitrite can come from metabolic or dietary sources, and from the medium itself, so a stimulus-free control and a medium-only control are essential for interpreting results.

## 4. Standard curve

Prepare a fresh nitrite standard series from Component C (1.0 mM). Use the **same matrix** for standards as your samples where possible (for example, fresh culture medium rather than water if you are measuring culture supernatant), because medium components can change background absorbance.

### Example dilution series

First make a **100 uM** working stock: 100 uL Component C + 900 uL diluent. Then serially dilute 1:2 (500 uL into 500 uL diluent):

| Standard | Nitrite (uM) |
| --- | --- |
| S1 | 100 |
| S2 | 50 |
| S3 | 25 |
| S4 | 12.5 |
| S5 | 6.25 |
| S6 | 3.13 |
| S7 | 1.56 |
| S8 (blank) | 0 (diluent only) |

Run every standard in **triplicate**. Standards are processed exactly like samples, using the same volume (150 uL per well for the microplate assay, 300 uL for the cuvette assay).

## 5. Microplate assay (recommended for multiple samples)

**Per well, total volume 300 uL:**

| Reagent | Volume |
| --- | --- |
| Deionized water | 130 uL |
| Griess Reagent | 20 uL |
| Sample **or** nitrite standard | 150 uL |

### Procedure

1. Plan the plate layout. Place standards (triplicate), blank, and samples (at least triplicate) on the same plate, and note the layout in your lab notebook.
2. Add 130 uL deionized water to every well that will hold a sample or standard.
3. Add 150 uL of sample or standard to the appropriate wells.
4. Prepare the Griess Reagent fresh (Section 2) and add **20 uL to each well**. Mix gently by pipetting or a brief plate shake; avoid bubbles.
5. Incubate for **30 minutes at room temperature**, protected from light.
6. Prepare the **photometric reference** by mixing 20 uL Griess Reagent with 280 uL deionized water in a separate well.
7. Read absorbance at **548 nm**, relative to the reference well. If your instrument cannot blank against a reference, subtract the reference well's absorbance from every reading.

## 6. Cuvette assay (alternative for single samples)

**Per cuvette (1 cm path length):**

| Reagent | Volume |
| --- | --- |
| Deionized water | 2.6 mL |
| Griess Reagent | 100 uL |
| Sample **or** nitrite standard | 300 uL |

### Procedure

1. Mix the three components above in a cuvette.
2. Incubate for **30 minutes at room temperature**.
3. Prepare the **photometric reference**: 100 uL Griess Reagent + 2.9 mL deionized water.
4. Measure the absorbance of each sample at **548 nm** relative to the reference.
5. Repeat the procedure for every standard in the dilution series.

## 7. Measuring nitrate (optional)

Nitrate does not react in the Griess assay. To quantify it, it must first be converted quantitatively to nitrite. Options named in the kit sheet:

- Enzymatic reduction with **nitrate reductase**
- In-line reduction by passing the sample through a column of copper-plated cadmium filings (automated analysis)

After reduction, proceed as above. Nitrate concentration is obtained from the total nitrite measured after reduction, minus the nitrite measured in an unreduced aliquot of the same sample.

## 8. Data analysis

1. Average the triplicate readings for each standard and subtract the 0 uM blank.
2. Plot **nitrite concentration (x-axis) against absorbance (y-axis)**.
3. Fit a linear regression. Record the equation (y = mx + c) and R-squared. Good curves should have R-squared close to 1; if the top standards plateau, exclude them and report the narrower range.
4. For each sample, subtract the blank, then calculate nitrite concentration from the regression.
5. Multiply by any dilution factor applied to the sample before the assay.
6. Report the mean and standard deviation (or SEM, stating which) across replicates, with n.
7. Samples above 100 uM should be diluted and re-run; samples below about 1 uM are at or below the detection limit and should be reported as such.

## 9. Controls checklist

- Blank: diluent only (0 uM)
- Reference: Griess Reagent + water
- Standard curve, 1-100 uM, triplicate, on every plate
- Medium-only control (nitrite already present in the medium)
- Negative biological control (no stimulus, empty-vector or uninduced strain)
- Positive control where available

## 10. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | What to try |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High background in blank | Old or oxidized Component A; mixed reagent stored too long | Mix fresh reagent; check Component A is sealed and stored cold and dark |
| Weak or no color in standards | Reagent degraded; wrong wavelength; standards badly diluted | Remake standards from Component C; confirm 548 nm (or 520-590 nm) |
| Non-linear curve | Absorbance saturating at high nitrite | Narrow the range or dilute samples |
| Samples read above the curve | Nitrite exceeds 100 uM | Dilute sample and re-run, applying the dilution factor |
| Noisy replicates | Bubbles, pipetting error, condensation | Re-pipette carefully; centrifuge the plate briefly; check for bubbles before reading |
| Sample color or turbidity interferes | Colored medium or cell debris | Re-clarify the sample; blank against sample plus water without reagent |

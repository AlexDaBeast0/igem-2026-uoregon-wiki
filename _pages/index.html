Preserve your ecosystem.

Recapture nitrogen pollution.

Mention Oregon. Today's agriculture relies on nitrogen fertilizer. Excess fertilized leaches into surface and ground water, harming the ecosystem and humans. We are engineering E. coli to sequester nitrate from water, store it as cyanophycin to be collected and reused as fertilizer.

~~~ In the present day ~~~
The modern farmer applies nitrogen-based fertilizer to their fields in hopes of a strong crop yield.
The issue is that between 10-80% of applied nitrogen can be **washed away** in agricultural runoff, making its way into subsurface drainage (9-18 ppm NO3- compared to the threshold 10 ppm) and privately owned wells (_____ ppm). One in every five private wells has nitrate levels above the threshold.

The modern day scientist wonders...
‘What if we could engineer bacteria to recapture nitrogen pollution in agricultural wastewater?’
In response, the University of Oregon iGEM team is engineering E. coli bacteria to
1. Uptake high levels of nitrogen in agricultural runoff ← this text goes where ‘text’ is on the drawing below
2. Store it inside the cell as the nitrogen-rich molecule, cyanophycin.
and 3. Flocculate in the presence of high levels of cyanophycin in order to be collected and recycled into nitrogen-rich fertilizer.
Our bacteria would then be implemented in filter systems at our two target wastewater streams.

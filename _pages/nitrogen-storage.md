After identifying nitrate to be the main contributor to our problem, the first idea was to use bacteria capable of capturing this nitrate. However, following an extensive literature review, we found that this approach wouldn’t be very novel nor effective by itself. A common denominator with solutions dealing with nitrate runoff was that they would simply expel the nitrate into a benign form, such as nitrogen gas. Furthermore, possible solutions such as riparian buffers, anion exchange systems, distillation, and reverse osmosis systems are expensive, ineffective, time and labor intensive, and don’t last long.

This stemmed the idea to recycle the nitrogen from this incoming excess nitrate. Nitrate wont be able to stably reside in the cell long enough for recycling the nitrogen content. For this to work, the nitrate needs to be stored in a stable enough form to be harvested and used as a fertilizer product. After searching and narrowing down various options, cyanophycin was deemed the best form of nitrogen storage. Cyanophycin is a granule polypeptide consisting of multiple linked dipeptides consisting of the amino acids, arginine and aspartate (). Cyanobacteria have utilized cyanophycin for storing nitrogen during periods of nitrogen fixation and nutrient stress, so it was perfect for storing the excess nitrogen from the incoming nitrate.
